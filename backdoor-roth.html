Non-Deductible Contribution
Contribute to a traditional IRA (no income limit on contributing; income limits apply only to the deduction, IRC 219(g)). Report it as nondeductible on Form 8606, Part I, which establishes basis.
2
Convert to Roth
Convert the traditional IRA to a Roth IRA and report it on Form 8606, Part II. Conversions have no income limit (IRC 408A(c)(3), (c)(5)(B)) and cannot be recharacterized (IRC 408A(d)(6)(B)(iii)).
3
Pro-Rata Calculation
If you hold other pre-tax traditional, SEP or SIMPLE IRA money at year-end, the pro-rata rule taxes part of the conversion. All IRAs are one contract. IRC 408(d)(2).

Eliminating the Pro-Rata Problem
If pre-tax IRA balances create pro-rata exposure, consider rolling them into your current employer 401(k) if the plan accepts rollover contributions. This removes them from line 6 of Form 8606 so the conversion is mostly or entirely tax-free. Complete the rollover before December 31 of the conversion year, because line 6 is measured at year-end (IRC 408(d)(2)(C)).
